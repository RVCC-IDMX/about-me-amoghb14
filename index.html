Blog 1: hover effects

Building interactive web pages is important for creating engaging user experiences. Hover effects are a great way to add interactivity and visual feedback to a website. Hover effects occur when an image or element visually changes after the user places their cursor over it. The effects can range from adding a border, border-radius, zooming in, fading out, and even color shifts. These are enacted typically to enhance user engagement and usability within applications.

It is far easier to enhance user experience with hover effects because changes on the webpages are visually appealing and immediate for the user. Especially with buttons and things that are to be clicked, adding hover effects can give the user a sense of assurance that they do have their cursor over the correct element.

However, in most practical applications, hover effects are very subtle rather than very flashy. Small changes can communicate the interactivity without being too flashy and completely distracting the user from other crucial elements of the site. In most professional applications, hover effects are used to reveal additional information. For example, with an online store, hovering over the product may add additional information about the product, which can help the user decide on their purchases. These interactions allow the web page designers to provide more information to the users without overcrowding the page and taking away from other static elements on the page.

Hover effects are also very practical because they do not involve nor require a lot of computational power. All the hover effects are handled directly within a user’s browser. They also do not require a lot of heavy scripting. Hover effects can usually just be taken care of through the CSS script. Because of how easy it is to use these effects, they are widely used in many web applications.

However, with hover effects, it is also important to considerthe diversity of users and different devices. When implementing hover effects, it is important to consider users who are on touchscreen devices. Since most of the time hover effects are toggled through interaction between an element and the cursor, users of an application on a mobile device ,like a phone, may not be able to interact with the webpage elements in the same way. Because of this, webpage navigation should not be completely dictated by the hover effects because it can discriminate against certain users. Some of the most commonly used hover effects are scale & zoom, opacity & fade, as well a color. Scale and zoom effects are used to increase or decrease the size of an image or element when the user hovers over it. Opacity and fade effects are used to change the transparency and opacity of a web element. This can often result in somewhat of a color change, which once again grabs the attention of the user. This effect is sometimes used to reveal text, icons, or layered images on top of one another. Color effects are used to directly change an image or element's appearance by changing the color completely(one of a different saturation and brightness). This is usually done to create contrast.

Ultimately, image hover properties are essential in web design. For cursor-based device users, it improves visual connection to certain elements and can even be used to embed additional information without detracting from the main page.

Blog 2: Background image properties

Background image properties are used to set an image as the background of a web page or element. These properties include background-image, background-repeat, background-position, and background-size. Background-image is typically and commonly used to specify the image to be used as the background. Background-repeat is used in a webpage to control how the image is repeated. Background-position is used to control where the image is positioned within the element. Background-size is used to determine how large or small the image appears.

Background images are usually controlled entirely through syling properties as opposed to regular images. This allows designers to manage the appearance of the image withough really altering the HTML script. One of the most properties of backgroud images is the control over its size and also how they are positioned within the larger page. Websites are accessed from a variety of devices with a wide range of screen sizes and dimensions. Making the background image adaptable to all of those scalings and positions is very important. the Background image properties allow the images to dynamically adjust to the webpages.

Background images are also used to create immersive sections, divide two separate parts into one main webpage, and decorate certain sections. They do not really interfere with text flow on a page, and designers can easily manipulate the image to make text or other elements show up more clearly over that image. Manipulating the images in this way allows designers to enrich designs while maintaining a design’s structure and flow. When implemented in the right ways, background images can actually enhance the readability of text or an understanding of a web page’s layout. A significant advantage of the background image and its properties is control over the repetition of the image and how much of the page it can cover. Some images are purposefully designed to repeat over a certain span on the page. This can create a pattern and textured feel to the webpage. Sometimes, an image is designed to only appear once as a focal element or simple background. The background image itself has properties to control these visual effects to cater to an intended requirement from the developer.

Background images also play a role in branding. When large corporations and entities design a site, the background image usually unifies the colors and scheme of the entire page. It can easily dictate the tone that the website sets at first glance. It's the difference between a futuristic and warm feeling website. The background image ultimately can be used and manipulated to dictate mood. From a maintenance and computation power perspective, when a background needs image repeating, using the repeat property can be much easier than including hte same image many times in the script directly. Ultimately, background image properties offer ways for web designers to set the tone and mood fo their application. It allows them to dictate the way that users interact with the page and differentiate between different sections and design elements. It plays a very important role in impacting web page layout, accessibility, and maintainability.
